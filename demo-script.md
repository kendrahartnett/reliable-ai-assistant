# The Deets — 2-Minute Demo

**Have open:** The Deets in one tab, the Test 7 screenshots in another, and the live input below copied.

---

### 1. What it does and who it's for (20 sec)

"This is **The Deets**, an assistant that turns any pasted text into a short summary and three key points, **for me to use on emails, articles and readings**. Its tagline is *'The meaning matters. Every detail counts,'* because it keeps the dates, numbers and conditions exactly as written."

### 2. Run it live (45 sec)

Click **"Just the deets, please. Here's the text:"** and paste:

> The Lakeside Community Center will offer free tax-preparation help on Saturdays from February 7 through April 11. Appointments are required, and only residents with a household income of up to $67,000 are eligible. Walk-ins may be accepted if volunteers are available. Bring photo ID and last year's tax return.

"Every time, you get the same structure: a ☑️ Summary and exactly three ⭐ Key Points, each 10 words or fewer. And look, it kept '**only**,' '**up to** $67,000' and '**may**… **if**.' Those are the words that change who qualifies."

### 3. The failure mode (40 sec) ⭐

Show the Test 7 screenshot.

"Here's where it breaks. On a longer passage, the source said priority goes to residents who haven't taken a **library digital-skills course**. The Deets wrote '**eligible first-time participants**,' which changes who qualifies, and it dropped a library-card requirement.

It looked perfect, and an AI evaluator even passed it. I only caught it by checking it against the source myself. My own 10-word rule forced it to compress.

**My mitigation:** a rule to keep essential qualifiers even when it has to shorten, plus a note telling users to check the original for anything high-stakes."

### 4. One design choice I'm proud of (15 sec)

"When formatting kept failing, I stopped *describing* the format and *showed* it with a literal template. That fixed it. It taught me that showing a model beats telling it."
